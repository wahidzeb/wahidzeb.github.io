Skyfire Squadron looks like a Saturday-morning cartoon. Chubby planes zoom over turquoise water, little islands with palm trees drift past, and when something is hit it bursts into a puff of cartoon smoke. There are no people on screen and nothing gory, so it suits younger players who like fast arcade action.

## How to fly

Tap **Play**, then pick a mission on the Missions map. Only mission 1-1 is open at first. Each win opens the next one.

Once the mission starts, **drag anywhere on the screen** to steer. The plane moves with your finger, so you do not have to cover it with your thumb. You never press a fire button, because **your guns shoot automatically** the whole time. Your job is to line up under enemies and get out of the way of their bullets.

Two buttons sit in the bottom-right corner:

- **BOMB** drops a bomb a short way in front of your plane. Use it on ships and island guns that your bullets have trouble finishing off. There is a short reload between bombs.
- **MEGA** is your emergency button. It wipes every enemy bullet off the screen in a big flash and gives you a moment of safety. You start each mission with one Mega charge.

On a keyboard, **B** drops a bomb, **Space** fires a Mega and **P** pauses. The pause button in the top-left corner works too.

The green bar at the top is your health. Each enemy bullet that reaches you takes one point away. The Sparrow, the first plane, starts with 6 health. If the bar runs out, you see **Shot Down!**

## Our first missions

Our first attempt at 1-1 lasted under a minute. We swept the plane left and right across the bottom of the screen without watching the bullets closely. We shot down 38 of 59 enemies but lost all our health. The second try ended the same way, with 36 of 49 enemies down, and so did a third, with 42 of 59. We have not yet won 1-1 playing by hand.

The lesson was simple: **watching the red bullets matters more than chasing planes.** Your guns hit anything in front of you, so small sideways moves that keep you out of the bullet streams pay off more than big swoops.

Every run still paid out: you keep the coins you collect even when you are shot down. Our third try earned 85 coins with no stars, so a failed mission still helps toward your first upgrade.

Mission 1-1 lasts a little under a minute. Later missions run longer and enemies fire faster.

## How the three stars work

Every results screen shows three goals. Each one is worth a star:

1. **Finish the mission.** A win always gives at least one star.
2. **Shoot down 70% of enemies.** The screen shows this as "enemies down", for example 38 / 59 on our first try.
3. **Keep half your health.** End the mission with at least 50% of your health bar.

Get all three and the title reads **Perfect Mission!** Otherwise it says **Mission Complete!** If you are shot down you get no stars, but you keep the coins you picked up.

Stars matter because the bigger planes need a minimum number of stars before you can buy them. You can always replay a mission to improve its stars. The game keeps your best result.

## Power-ups

Some enemies, especially big ones and cargo ships, drop a floating power-up when destroyed. Fly into it to grab it:

- **Power up!** makes your guns stronger, up to five levels. Once you are at the top level, extra ones give 25 coins instead.
- **Shield!** blocks hits for about nine seconds.
- **Wingmen!** adds two helper planes that fly beside you and shoot for about twenty seconds.
- **Repair!** gives back 3 health.
- **Magnet!** pulls coins toward you from much further away for a while.
- **+1 Mega bomb** adds another Mega charge.

Repair is the one to chase when your health bar is getting low, because it can be the difference between one star and three.

## Coins, the Hangar and upgrades

You collect coins from beaten enemies, plus a bonus for winning that grows with each star. Spend them in the **Hangar**. It has two parts.

**Upgrades** apply to every plane you own. Each level costs more than the last:

| Upgrade | What it does | First level | Max level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guns | +12% gun damage | 120 coins | 10 |
| Armor | +1 health | 150 coins | 10 |
| Bombs | Bigger, faster bombs | 130 coins | 10 |
| Mega Bomb | +1 Mega charge per mission | 300 coins | 4 |
| Wingmen | Start with wingmen, more firepower | 400 coins | 5 |
| Magnet | Pull coins from further away | 100 coins | 6 |

**Planes** are unlocked with coins and stars together:

| Plane | Coins | Stars needed |
|---|---|---|
| Sparrow | Free | – |
| Hornet | 1,200 | 8 |
| Pelican | 2,600 | 20 |
| Falcon | 5,200 | 38 |
| Phoenix | 9,500 | 60 |

The Sparrow is described as "Trusty twin guns. Easy to fly." In the Hangar each plane shows bars for Firepower, Speed, Armor and Bombs, so you can compare before buying. When you have enough coins and stars for a new plane, a message after a mission tells you so.

Armor is a good first upgrade for younger players, because one extra health point lets you survive one more mistake. Guns is next, since stronger guns finish waves faster and leave fewer bullets in the air.

## Stages and bosses

There are **32 missions**, eight in each of four stages. The map shows a warning sign on mission 4 of each stage and a skull on mission 8:

| Stage | Mission 4 mini-boss | Mission 8 boss |
|---|---|---|
| Sunny Seas | Heavy Cruiser | Ironjaw Dreadnought |
| Palm Isles | Grumble Bomber | Big Mama Carrier |
| Frosty Fjords | Heavy Cruiser | Frostbite Icebreaker |
| Thunder Night | Grumble Bomber | Storm Baron |

When a boss arrives the screen flashes **WARNING!** and a boss health bar appears. Big ships carry turrets. In the early boss fights the game gives a tip to **knock out the turrets first, then sink it**, and that is good advice for all of them. Fewer turrets means fewer bullets coming your way. Save your Mega charge for the moment the boss fills the screen with fire.

Beating the boss on mission 8 unlocks the next stage. Beating the Storm Baron at the end of Thunder Night brings up the message "You beat the Storm Baron! Ace pilot!"

We have only played the first mission of Sunny Seas so far, so the later stages are described from the game's own mission data rather than from our play.

## Daily reward

The **Daily** button on the title screen gives free coins once a day. It starts at 100 coins. Come back on following days to build a streak, and the reward grows each day up to 1,000 coins on day seven, then the cycle starts again. A red badge on the button shows when today's reward is waiting.

## Tips for new pilots

- Stay near the bottom of the screen. It gives you the most time to see bullets coming.
- Make small moves. A short slide sideways is usually enough to dodge.
- Use bombs on ships. Gunfire works, but bombs finish ships faster.
- Do not hoard Mega. A Mega used to save three health points is better than one you never fire.
- Replay early missions for stars. Three-star runs earn bigger coin bonuses and count toward the next plane.
- Buy Armor early, then Guns.

## Is Skyfire Squadron good for kids?

Yes. It is a cartoon shooter with planes, ships and puffs of smoke, and no people are shown being hurt. Missions are short, the controls are a single finger, and you can always retry. The difficulty rises steadily through the stages, so younger players may want help with the later bosses. Progress is saved on your device.
