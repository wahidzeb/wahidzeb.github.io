Sky Glide looks like a page from a school sketchbook. The first world is a **classroom**, with a chalkboard (someone has written E = mc² on it), a globe on a cupboard, ceiling lamps and giant pencils poking up from the floor and down from the ceiling. Your paper plane leaves a little trail behind it as it swoops through.

It has one control, no shooting and no scary bits, which makes it a nice pick for younger players as well as anyone who likes a calm challenge.

## Our first flight

We chose **Adventure**, then level **1-1** in the Classroom. Instead of holding the button for a long time, we used **short taps**: hold for about a quarter of a second, let go for a bit longer, and repeat. The plane bobbed up and down in a gentle wave.

At about 186 metres we slipped between a purple pencil hanging from the ceiling and a green one standing on the floor, and a **"NICE! +50"** popped up: a **near miss**. By the end of the 680-metre level we had 3 near misses, a best combo of **x2**, 1 coin and a score of 890. **LEVEL CLEAR!** and 11 coins for the hangar.

The lesson: **rhythm beats reaction.** Long holds send the plane shooting up into the ceiling; steady short taps keep it level and let you make small corrections.

| Pause | Pause button, top right | P or Esc |

## Near misses and combos

Passing **close to an obstacle without touching it** gives a near-miss bonus. Near misses in a row build a **combo multiplier up to x8**. It is tempting to chase them, but crashing into things costs hearts, so go for near misses only when the gap is wide enough.

## Stars, coins and goals

- Each level has **3 hidden stars**. The progress bar at the top shows three star outlines that fill in as you find them.
- **Coins** float in lines and arcs. Following a coin line is often the safest path too.
- **Clearing a level** unlocks the next one. Each world has 4 levels, and finishing the 4th unlocks the next world.
- **14 goals** pay out bonus coins.

## The five worlds

| World | Obstacles | Special physics |
|---|---|---|
| Classroom | Pencils, books and ceiling lamps | Normal |
| Sunny Park | Trees, birds and kite strings | Normal |
| City Rooftops | Towers, cranes and clotheslines | Normal |
| Stormy Skies | Lightning, hail and turbines | Gusts and downdrafts push you around |
| Outer Space | Asteroids, UFOs and black holes | Low gravity, so you fall slowly |

Each world has its own art and music. In Stormy Skies, tap a little more firmly to fight the downdrafts. In space, tap less, because the plane floats.

## Endless mode

**Endless** flies you through all five worlds in a row with rising speed, and saves your best distance. It is a good way to practise the later worlds before you unlock them in Adventure.

## What changed after testing

Saves were made safer and values are checked when they load. Pop-up messages were turned into a neat pill shape that sits well on phones held upright, and the share button was moved so it never covers the menu.

## FAQ

### Is Sky Glide hard?
The first world is gentle. Later worlds add wind and low gravity, which take practice.

### Should I hold or tap?
Use short taps. Long holds make the plane climb too fast.

### How many stars are there?
60: three in each of the 20 levels.

### Is my progress saved?
Yes, on your device.
