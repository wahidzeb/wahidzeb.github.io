A good quick-break game has three things. You can start playing within seconds, a round or level is over in a few minutes, and when you stop, nothing is lost. All eight games below tick those boxes. Each one keeps your coins, stars and unlocked levels on the phone, tablet or computer you play on, so you can pick up where you left off next time on the same device.

We have grouped them by how much thinking they ask for, from "just tap" to "let me plan this one".

## One tap and go

These are the games to open when you want to switch your brain off for a moment. They have one main control and you will understand them in a few seconds.

### Neon Dash

[Neon Dash](play:neon-dash) is a glowing runner where your cube runs by itself and you only decide when to jump. That is the whole control: tap to jump. Each level has a fixed layout that never changes, so every try teaches you a little more about where the spikes are.

Runs are short and the Retry button is right there on the results card, which makes it perfect for "one more go". There are 200 levels, so you will not run out. Our first two tries at level 1 ended at 21% and 29%, and that is completely normal.

**Quick-break tip:** aim for "past the next block" rather than "finish the level". Small goals fit small breaks. The game also pauses itself if you switch away, so a sudden interruption will not cost you the run.

More help: [Neon Dash guide](guide:neon-dash).

### Sky Glide

[Sky Glide](play:sky-glide) is the calm cousin of Neon Dash. You fly a paper plane through hand-drawn worlds: hold to rise, let go to glide. There is no shooting and nothing scary, so it suits younger players too. The first level in the Classroom is a 680-metre flight between giant pencils, which takes only a short while once you get the hang of it.

**Quick-break tip:** use short, steady taps. Hold for about a quarter of a second, let go for a little longer, and repeat. Long holds send the plane shooting into the ceiling. Short taps keep it level.

More help: [Sky Glide guide](guide:sky-glide).

## Games with a built-in timer

If you only have a couple of minutes, a game with its own clock is ideal, because it decides when the round ends for you.

### Gobble Hole

In [Gobble Hole](play:gobble-hole) you are a hole in the ground. Slide under cones, benches, people and cars to swallow them and grow, until you are big enough to sink whole towers. A Classic round lasts exactly **two minutes**, and you play against seven computer holes to be the biggest when time runs out. Solo mode also lasts two minutes, but there is no one else: you try to eat as much of the map as you can, with 1 star for half the map, 2 stars for 80% and 3 stars for all of it.

**Quick-break tip:** start on the pavements, where small things are packed close together, and keep away from bigger holes early on. Being swallowed sends you back at half your size. That is exactly how we lost our first round.

More help: [Gobble Hole guide](guide:gobble-hole).

### Juicy Merge (Timed mode)

[Juicy Merge](play:juicy-merge) is a fruit-dropping puzzle. Drop a fruit into the jar, and when two identical fruits touch they merge into a bigger one, all the way up to a Watermelon. The classic game can go on for a long time if you are tidy, so for a quick break try **Timed** mode. You start with 90 seconds, and making an Orange or bigger adds a little extra time (an Orange adds 2 seconds, a Watermelon adds 7).

**Quick-break tip:** in Timed mode, speed matters more than neatness. Keep your big fruits in one bottom corner, drop small ones on the other side, and do not stop to think too long about each drop.

More help: [Juicy Merge guide](guide:juicy-merge).

## A little puzzle, a little thinking

These games ask you to stop and think for a moment, but each level is still short. They are great for a break that feels a bit like a brain stretch.

### Number Dash

[Number Dash](play:number-dash) is a running game where the puzzle is maths. Your little Buddy runs down a track, and you steer through gates that add, take away, multiply or divide. At the end, a Number Monster is waiting, and you win if your number is bigger. There are 60 levels across six worlds, from Sunny Meadow to Starry Space, and the levels are short.

**Quick-break tip:** adding wins when your number is small, and multiplying wins when it is big. If you have 3, a +10 gate beats a ×2 gate. If you have 30, the ×2 is better. And watch the gates near the finish, because a red one at the end can undo a great start.

More help: [Number Dash guide](guide:number-dash).

### Kedim's Snack Swing

[Kedim's Snack Swing](play:kedim-snack-swing) is a rope-cutting puzzle with a very hungry orange cat. Swipe the ropes, swing the fish treat past the stars and drop it into Kedim's mouth. Our very first level took one swipe. Later levels add bubbles, spikes and clever gadgets, but each one is still a single small puzzle you can solve in a minute or two.

**Quick-break tip:** watch a full swing before you touch anything. Most levels show you the answer if you look at where the treat goes. Restarting costs nothing, and every level can be solved. If you are truly stuck, the light bulb replays the full solution.

More help: [Kedim's Snack Swing guide](guide:kedim-snack-swing).

### Block Glide

[Block Glide](play:block-glide) is a calm sliding puzzle. Chunky clay blocks sit on a small board, and you slide each one out through a gate of its own colour. Level 1 takes a single move. Later levels need you to work out which block has to leave first so the others can get past.

**Quick-break tip:** on timed levels, the clock only starts on your first move. Take as long as you like to look at the board, then move. That makes it a relaxing pick even when there is a timer.

More help: [Block Glide guide](guide:block-glide).

## A calm one for winding down

### Find Kedim!

[Find Kedim!](play:find-kedim) is a hidden cat game. Kedim and lots of friends are hiding in 15 busy hand-drawn pictures. Drag to look around, zoom in and tap every cat you see. A full picture can take longer than five minutes, but that does not matter: your finds are saved after every single cat, so you can stop in the middle of a picture and carry on later.

**Quick-break tip:** if a cat refuses to show itself, tap the glowing magnifier. It glides to a cat you have not found yet and marks the spot. Hints never run out, they just need a few seconds to fill up again.

More help: [Find Kedim! guide](guide:find-kedim).

## Which one should I pick?

Here is a quick way to choose, depending on how much time and energy you have:

| If you have... | Try |
|---|---|
| One minute, and you want to just tap | Neon Dash or Sky Glide |
| Exactly two minutes | Gobble Hole (Classic or Solo) |
| About 90 seconds and fast fingers | Juicy Merge, Timed mode |
| A few minutes and want to think | Number Dash, Snack Swing or Block Glide |
| A few minutes and want to relax | Find Kedim! |

## Tips for healthy quick breaks

Short games are best when they stay short. A few ideas that work for us, and for families too:

1. **Decide your number of rounds before you start.** "Three Gobble Hole rounds" is easy to stick to. "Until I win" is not.
2. **Turn the sound down in public.** Many of our games, Neon Dash included, let you switch the music off in Settings.
3. **Rest your eyes.** After a few rounds, look at something far away for a moment before you go back to work or homework.
4. **For children, pick the calm ones near bedtime.** Find Kedim! and Block Glide are gentle. Neon Dash is fast and flashy, so it is better earlier in the day.
