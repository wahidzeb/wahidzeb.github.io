Two Players, One Phone: Our Games You Can Play Together

Some of the best gaming moments happen with two people huddled around one screen. Four of our games have a proper two-player mode on a single phone or tablet, with no second device and no internet match-making. Here is how each one works.

By Zaib · 20 October 2026

Playing with someone sitting right next to you is a different kind of fun. You can see their face when they miss, you can shout "behind you!", and a rematch is one tap away. Four games on Games by Zaib are built for exactly that: two players sharing one phone, tablet or computer.

Each one splits the screen in its own way, so it helps to know the setup before you start. Here is a quick guide.

Goal Frenzy: a quick football match

Goal Frenzy is our arcade football game, with a big stadium, curling shots and over-the-top power shots such as the Fire Shot and Banana Curve. Matches are short and fast.

In the two-player mode each person controls their team from their own half of the screen. On a phone, Player 1 gets a joystick and buttons on the left half, and Player 2 gets their own set on the right half. Move with the joystick and play with SHOOT and PASS. Hold SHOOT for more power and swipe across it to curl the ball. Without the ball, SHOOT becomes a slide tackle and PASS switches to another player. On a computer, Player 1 uses W A S D and Player 2 uses the arrow keys.

Tips for a fair game: - A tablet, or a phone turned sideways, gives each player more room for their thumbs. - Try 5-a-side first. It is easier to follow than 7-a-side. - Agree on a time limit before you start, or the final turns into "next goal wins" forever.

Shuttle Smash: badminton across the table

Shuttle Smash is a badminton game where your player runs to the shuttle by itself. You only decide where and how hard to hit it, by swiping. A short swipe plays a gentle drop shot near the net, a long swipe sends the shuttle high to the back, and a long swipe on a high shuttle is a jump smash.

The two-player mode is the most "board game" of the four. Lay the phone flat on a table and sit facing each other. Player 1 swipes on the bottom half of the screen and Player 2 on the top half. It feels like a tiny badminton court between you.

Tips: - Mix short and long shots. A drop shot after two long clears catches people out every time. - The power button fills during long rallies. Save it for a point that matters, then unleash a Thunder Smash or Tornado Shot. - Choose a shorter match length for a quick game: as well as the full 21 points, you can play to 11 or 15.

Noodle Clash: snakes, side by side

Noodle Clash is a snake battle in a giant noodle bowl. You eat glowing food to grow longer, and any snake whose head touches another snake's body is out. The game's computer-controlled snakes have real personalities: some hunt, some run away, and some try to coil around you.

The two-player mode is called Duo. The screen splits down the middle: Player 1 steers on the left half with their own joystick and boost button, and Player 2 does the same on the right. You both play in the same arena, along with a crowd of computer snakes. A Duo round lasts three minutes; if you crash, you come back, and whoever is longest when the timer runs out wins. Duo needs the phone turned sideways, and the game asks you to rotate it if you start upright.

Tips: - Boosting is fast, but it costs length and leaves a trail of food behind you, which your friend might eat. - The big bright orbs come from snakes that crashed, so a crash nearby means a feast. - The red border is deadly, so don't chase food into the edge of the bowl.

Meowstronauts: two cats, one mission

Meowstronauts is different from the other three because you play on the same team. Sunny and Shadow, the two space cats, fly across six planets to get home, and in 2 Players each of you controls one cat.

Player 1 drags on the left half of the screen and Player 2 on the right half. Both cats shoot by themselves, so you can focus on dodging and collecting fish stars. If one cat gets knocked out, they float in a bubble, and the other player can fly into the bubble to rescue them. Reaching any planet together earns the Best Friends mission.

Tips: - Split the jobs: one player collects fish stars while the other keeps the space mice busy. - Talk to each other before using your powers. Sunny's Sun Shield and Shadow's Shadow Cloak work best at different moments. - The game suggests turning the phone sideways, and in two-player mode this really helps, since each player gets a wider half.

Setting up for two players

A few things make shared-screen games more comfortable for everyone:

  1. Turn the phone sideways for Goal Frenzy, Noodle Clash and Meowstronauts. Each player gets a wider half.
  2. Use a table for Shuttle Smash. Holding the phone between two people is tiring. Flat on a table works best.
  3. A tablet is even better. All four games work on tablets, and the extra space makes a big difference with four thumbs on one screen.
  4. Turn the sound up. Hearing the crowd, the shuttle and the crashes adds a lot to the fun.
  5. Take turns choosing. One person picks the game, the other picks the settings. It keeps things fair.

Which one to pick

  • For a quick, loud match: Goal Frenzy.
  • For a calm, back-and-forth game at the table: Shuttle Smash.
  • For chaos and laughter: Noodle Clash Duo.
  • When you would rather help each other than compete: Meowstronauts.

All four are free, play right in your browser with no download, and work on phones, tablets and computers.
