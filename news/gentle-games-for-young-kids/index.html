No Timers, No Losing: Five Gentle Games for Younger Children

Not every game needs a countdown or a game-over screen. Five of our games are built so younger children can play at their own pace, without anything scary and without ever really losing. Here is what each one is like and why parents tend to like them too.

By Zaib · 20 October 2026

When we design a game for younger players, we ask a few simple questions. Can a child play it without reading much? Is there anything that might frighten or upset them? And what happens when they make a mistake? In the five games below, the answer to that last question is always kind. A plant just grows a little slower, a pet is a bit hungry, or a giggling raccoon sends you back to the start.

We played each of them properly for our guides, and these are the games we would hand to a five- or six-year-old with confidence. Older children and grown-ups enjoy them too, especially at the end of a long day.

Silly Sprout Garden: plants that turn into animals

Silly Sprout Garden has a pastel picture-book garden with a pink cottage, a pond and butterflies. You plant a seed, tap the sprout to water it, and when it wiggles and sparkles you tap again to harvest. The surprise is that every plant grows into a funny creature. Our very first harvest was a Lettuce Lamb: "Crunchy on the outside, cuddly on the inside."

There are 34 creatures to find, from the Common Carrot Cat and Pea Pup to the Mythic Galaxy Gourd Phoenix. The weather changes every minute or so, and special weather can make a creature Golden, Glowing or Rainbow. Nothing can wither or go wrong. Watering just makes plants grow faster, and a child can water again and again until the game says "Full of water!"

Why kids like it: the "NEW CREATURE!" moment never gets old. What it practises: patience, collecting and a little bit of simple saving up for better seeds.

Sunny Acres: a farm with no deadlines

Sunny Acres is a sunny patch of countryside with fields, a red barn and a little truck. You pick a seed and then swipe across the fields to plant them all in one go, which small fingers find very satisfying. Fifteen seconds later the wheat is ready to swipe again to harvest.

Villagers like Farmer Joe post orders on a board. When a green tick shows you have everything, you press Send and the truck drives off with your crops. As you level up you unlock chickens, cows and sheep, then buildings like the Bakery that turn wheat, eggs and milk into Cake. Crops keep growing while the game is closed.

Why parents like it: there is no fighting and no time pressure. Every item has an emoji picture, so reading is light. What it practises: simple planning, such as "if I plant corn now, I can feed the cows later."

Dream Room: be an interior designer

In Dream Room each level is a client with a wish. Our first was Mia: "My very first flat! Can you make the bedroom calm and cosy?" The brief shows her style, her favourite colours, a budget and a list of must-haves. You tap the glowing + signs in the room, pick a piece of furniture and choose its colour.

A little ♥ next to an item means this client will like it, which gives younger players an easy rule to follow. When the room is done you see a before-and-after reveal and the client's reaction. Ours earned 100% mood and three stars. There are 32 levels across five chapters, ending with multi-room Dream Estates.

Why kids like it: making things look nice, and the big reveal at the end. What it practises: reading a short brief, matching colours and keeping to a budget, which is a sneaky bit of maths.

Betta Bestie: a pet that is always happy to see you

Betta Bestie puts a round fishbowl in a cosy room. You name your betta, choose her colour and then pop in to feed her, wipe algae off the glass and play little mini-games such as Bubble Pop and Hoop Jump. Her three meters, full, clean and happy, drift down slowly in real time.

What makes it gentle is that the meters stop at a soft floor. In our long test we left her alone for five days. When we came back she was hungry, the glass was a bit green, and she said "I missed you! I'm a bit hungry." Nothing bad happened. There is also a built-in break: after about 300 coins from mini-games in a day she gets sleepy, and the game says so.

Why parents like it: short daily visits of five to ten minutes work best, and there is nothing to buy with real money. What it practises: gentle routine and caring for something.

Critter Keep Rescue: hide and seek with raccoons

Critter Keep Rescue is the most adventurous of the five, but it stays soft from top to bottom. You sneak through little hedge mazes to free critters from cages, while raccoon bandits in masks patrol with a yellow cone showing what they can see. Hide in a bush and they cannot spot you.

When a raccoon catches you, it just giggles, "Gotcha! Hehe!", and you go back to the start. You always finish the level in the end. Getting caught only changes how many stars you earn. Freed critters live in your keep, where they earn coins for eggs, and two of the same critter merge into a rarer one.

Why kids like it: sneaking feels exciting without being scary. What it practises: watching patterns and planning a route before moving.

A few tips for parents

  • Sit with them for the first five minutes. Each game has friendly on-screen hints, but early readers may want help with the first few.
  • Short sessions work best. Betta Bestie and Silly Sprout Garden are designed around quick visits, and both have daily gifts that reward coming back tomorrow.
  • Talk about the choices. "Why do you think Mia would like the green one?" turns Dream Room into a conversation.
  • Progress is saved on the device. If your child switches between a phone and a tablet, each one keeps its own save.

Where to start

For the very youngest players, try Silly Sprout Garden or Betta Bestie. They need only taps and have the least reading. For children who like a goal, Sunny Acres and Dream Room add orders and briefs to work towards. For those ready for a little excitement, Critter Keep Rescue brings sneaking and hiding with no real danger.

All five are free, play in the browser on phones, tablets and computers, and have full guides on our Guides page, with screenshots from our own play.
